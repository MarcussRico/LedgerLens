Why nobody catches it

It isn't that auditors are careless. It's that the evidence doesn't exist inside any one document.

The duplicate is a relationship between this invoice and one from three weeks ago. The overcharge is a relationship between this price and eleven other vendors'. The shell company is a relationship between a vendor's bank account and an employee's.

What already exists, and what doesn't

Scroll to §09 Novelty. The comparison table.

We didn't invent the statistics. Benford's law in forensic accounting is Nigrini's work. Isolation Forest is Liu, Ting and Zhou, 2008. The fraud numbers are ACFE's. We cite all of it.

And there are four categories of tool that already do parts of this. ERP controls enforce policy at entry — but a compliant purchase order that's one of five splitting a purchase is still compliant. Invoice-capture software reads a document beautifully; the document is its whole world. Audit analytics can run corpus-wide tests, but an analyst has to specify each one, per engagement. Spend dashboards aggregate to categories — which is exactly the operation that hides a pattern inside a total.

None of them is wrong. They're all built around a document. The frauds that cost the most don't live inside one.

So: four things that are genuinely ours. The unit of analysis is the relationship, not the record. Resolution runs before comparison, not as a feature. Our accuracy is measured against frauds we planted, not asserted. And the model is structurally forbidden from arithmetic.

Proof, and where we're weakest

Scroll to §10 Proof. The command is printed on screen.

We built a simulator that plants 150 labelled frauds of known type into realistic spend, then runs the engine blind. Because we know exactly what we planted, this is a measurement, not a claim.

90.2% precision. 98.7% recall. That command is on the slide — run it yourself, it's seeded, you'll get the same answer.

Two honest things. That recall is an upper bound: the simulator plants the kinds of fraud our detectors were built to find, so it proves they work on their targets, not that we've covered everything. Precision is the number to trust.

And our weakest pillar is duplicates, at ninety-one percent. Our transposition rule matches invoice numbers within two characters, and across a big corpus some genuinely different documents differ by that little. We could tighten it — and we'd lose the case where a vendor re-keys a bill, which is the exact thing the rule exists to catch. So we left it, and we're telling you.

It handles fifty thousand invoices in twelve and a half seconds. We are not the bottleneck; getting the data out of your ERP is.

If they push

"Is this actually running, or is it a mock-up?"
The demo dataset is bundled so it can't fail on venue wifi. The last tab is a live engine on a server — hand me a CSV and we'll run it now. Forty-two detectors, and the response includes a hash you can reproduce.
"How do I know the AI isn't making up the numbers?"
It's checked, not trusted. Every number in a drafted letter has to appear in the evidence the rules produced; if the model derives a figure — even correctly adding two invoices — we discard the letter. That check caught it doing exactly that during development.
"Your savings figure looks too good."
On the demo it's 0.43% of spend, below the published benchmark for duplicates alone. On the live sample it's higher and the system says so itself — it flags any total above 5% of spend as implausible and tells you to quote only the measured tiers.
"What if the client's own records are forged?"
There's a zero-trust mode. Nineteen of our forty-two detectors need no baseline at all — mathematical invariants, statutory checks, and structural facts like a shared bank account. Those still work when the history itself is fiction.
"Can you add a rule for X?"
A detector is one file and one decorator — that's the plugin design on slide 5. Tell me the rule and I'll write it tonight. Do not promise it inside the session; that sandbox isn't built.
"What's not finished?"
Open-ended natural language — the three questions in Ask are prepared, and the page says so. The feedback loop that reweights a detector from analyst labels is designed, not built. And ERP connectors are the real productionisation work.
